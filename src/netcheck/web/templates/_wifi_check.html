Check your Wi-Fi yourself (2 minutes)

No website can see inside your Wi-Fi. These are the checks that matter most, and you can do them from your router's app or admin page (the address and login are usually on a sticker on the router).

  1. Look at the list of connected devices. Find "Connected devices", "Attached devices" or "Clients". Anything you don't recognise? Change the Wi-Fi password: every device has to reconnect, and strangers get kicked off.
  2. Security type should be WPA2 or WPA3. If it says WEP, WPA (without the 2), or "Open", change it now.
  3. Change default passwords. Both the Wi-Fi password and the router's admin password. If either is still the one printed on the sticker and your router is old, anyone who's been near the sticker knows it.
  4. Turn off WPS. The push-button/PIN pairing feature is easy to attack on many routers.
  5. Turn off remote management (sometimes called "Remote access" or "Web access from WAN") unless you use it.
  6. Update the firmware, or turn on automatic updates. If your router hasn't had an update in years, replace it.

Signs that something may be wrong: your router's settings changed without you, web pages redirect to strange sites or show unexpected ads, or your internet is suddenly slow with nothing running.
